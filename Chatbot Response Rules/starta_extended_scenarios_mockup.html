What does ROE mean and why does it matter?
11:32 AM
S

Great question - ROE is one of my favorite metrics for evaluating management quality. Let me break it down.

📚 Return on Equity (ROE) - Definition

Formula: ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity

What it measures: How efficiently a company generates profit from the shareholders' money invested in the business. Think of it as: "For every EGP 100 of equity, how much profit does management generate?"

Example with Egyptian numbers:

Company A (JUFO):
• Net Income: EGP 2,735M
• Shareholders' Equity: EGP 7,084M
• ROE = 2,735 / 7,084 = 38.6%

Company B (Competitor):
• Net Income: EGP 1,200M
• Shareholders' Equity: EGP 6,500M
• ROE = 1,200 / 6,500 = 18.5%

Interpretation: JUFO generates EGP 38.60 of profit for every EGP 100 of shareholder equity. Company B only generates EGP 18.50. JUFO is using capital more than 2x as efficiently.

Why ROE Matters (The Real Insight):

💡 ROE Separates Great Businesses from Average Ones

High ROE (>20% in Egyptian context) tells you:

  • Management is disciplined about capital allocation
  • Company likely has competitive moats (pricing power, brand, economies of scale)
  • Business model is capital-efficient
  • Less need to raise external capital (can fund growth from profits)

Low ROE (<10%) often signals:

  • Commodity business with no competitive advantage
  • Poor capital allocation by management
  • Capital-intensive business model (needs constant reinvestment)
  • Struggling operations

Egyptian Market Context:

• Banks: Good ROE is 15-20% (CIB: ~25% = excellent)
• Consumer/F&B: Good ROE is 18-25% (JUFO: 38.6% = exceptional)
• Real Estate: Good ROE is 12-18% (capital intensive sector)
• Industrials: Good ROE is 12-16%

⚠️ When ROE Can Be Misleading

High leverage inflates ROE: Company can boost ROE by taking on debt. If equity is small but debt is huge, ROE looks great but business is risky.

Example: Company with EGP 1B equity, EGP 5B debt, makes EGP 300M profit → ROE = 30%. Looks great, but D/E ratio of 5x is dangerous. Always check ROE alongside leverage ratios.

One-time items distort it: Asset sale, accounting changes, or one-off gains can spike ROE temporarily. Look at 3-5 year average, not single year.

How I Use ROE in Stock Selection:

When screening Egyptian stocks, I look for:

  • ROE >15% (quality filter)
  • Consistent ROE (last 3-5 years) - not volatile
  • ROE above sector average (competitive advantage)
  • Combined with reasonable leverage (D/E <0.7x) - sustainable ROE

Real example from today's Egyptian market:

JUFO: ROE 38.6%, D/E 0.62x
→ High ROE with manageable leverage = genuine operating excellence
→ This is the gold standard - efficient capital use without excessive risk

Company X: ROE 35%, D/E 3.2x
→ High ROE but extreme leverage = looks good on surface, very risky
→ One bad quarter and equity gets wiped out

Company Y: ROE 8%, D/E 0.3x
→ Low ROE despite low leverage = poor business model or weak execution
→ Conservative balance sheet can't save bad operations

Bottom Line: ROE is one of the best single metrics for evaluating management quality and competitive positioning. But always pair it with leverage analysis. High ROE + low-to-moderate debt = truly exceptional business. High ROE + high debt = proceed with caution.
